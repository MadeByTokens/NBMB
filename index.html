    How it works, in full

    Why this page exists

    Some people worry that a name they search for could be spotted, bought first and resold to them at a profit. This page is built to reduce that risk, and it shows you exactly what it does so you can judge for yourself.

    What happens when you press Check domains
    1. Your browser runs this page’s code. There is no server of ours to send your ideas to.
    2. It finds the registry for the domain ending, using a list published by IANA that is stored in this page.
    3. It looks up a name known to be registered. This tests the registry without revealing your idea. If the test fails, your name is not sent.
    4. It sends your name straight to the registry over an encrypted connection.
    5. It shows the answer and who saw it, then forgets it. Nothing is saved.

    Open “Show what was sent” under a result to see each request. The option does not appear when nothing was sent. You can compare the list with your browser’s network tab, and read this page’s source.

    What we did to protect your ideas
    Skipped registrar search boxes
    Registrar search pages and free lookup sites can log what you type. Here, you do not use them. Some registries do share an owner with a registrar, as explained below.
    Skipped DNS checks unless you choose one
    A DNS check can pass through several outside systems, and a registered name can have no DNS entry. Asking the registry directly involves fewer parties. If a registry cannot be reached, you can choose Google DNS for that name.
    Removed our own access
    This page has no backend, analytics or outside scripts. Its security policy lets it connect only to the listed registries and Google DNS, so it cannot quietly send your ideas elsewhere.
    Reduced browser leaks
    This page asks your browser to turn off spellcheck, autocomplete and translation, and to skip its cache for lookups. Searches stay out of your browser history. Your browser and extensions may not honor these settings, and we have not audited what your browser reports to its maker.

    Who can still see your search
    The registry
    Sees the name, your IP address and which website you used. This cannot be avoided, because the registry holds the answer. A VPN hides your IP address.
    Network providers
    Some registries route lookups through a provider such as Cloudflare, which can read them. Results name the ones we know about, but the list may be incomplete.
    Companies that own a registry and a registrar
    Identity Digital, CentralNic and GoDaddy run registries and also have registrar businesses. We found no evidence that they misuse lookups, but we cannot see inside them. Results name them so you can decide.
    Google, only if you press Check through Google DNS
    Sees the name and your IP address, and keeps some of this in its logs. It then asks the servers for that ending without your IP address, and that request is not encrypted. Google sold its registrar business, but it still runs a registry.
    Your internet provider
    Sees which registry you contacted, but not the name.
    This website’s host
    Sees that you loaded the page, but not your search, as long as it serves this code unchanged. To avoid trusting the host, run the page on your own computer.
    Your browser extensions
    May see everything on this page. Use a private window with extensions turned off.

    This reduces the risk, but it cannot remove it. We cannot promise that nobody will ever see your search.

    When you find a name you like

    Buy it soon. This page cannot protect you from delay, and a name is only safe once it is yours.

    “No record found” is a good sign, not proof. Search at a registrar only when you are ready to pay, and only at the one you plan to use. It shows the real price and whether the name can be bought.

    A name with no record can still infringe a trademark, so search trademark records first.

    This page is for a person checking a few names. Registries limit lookups, ban bulk automated use, and may block your IP address if you run many.
